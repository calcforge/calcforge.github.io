How to Calculate CFM

CFM means cubic feet per minute and is a common unit for airflow. In HVAC work, CFM connects room volume, ventilation rates, duct design and fan performance.

1. Calculate CFM from air changes

When room volume and the required air changes per hour are known, a common relationship is CFM = room volume × ACH ÷ 60. Room volume is normally in cubic feet for this form of the equation. The result is the airflow required to achieve the stated air-change rate.

2. Check the room volume

Multiply room length × width × average ceiling height. If dimensions are supplied in metres, convert the volume consistently before using a cubic-feet equation, or use a metric airflow relationship instead. Mixed units are a common source of incorrect results.

3. Understand what the result means

A calculated CFM value is an airflow target for the stated assumption. It does not by itself determine equipment capacity. Cooling and heating loads, outdoor-air requirements, infiltration, occupancy, pressure losses and equipment performance may require a different design airflow.

4. Example

For a 20 ft × 15 ft room with an 8 ft average ceiling, volume is 2,400 ft³. At 4 air changes per hour, CFM = 2,400 × 4 ÷ 60 = 160 CFM. This is an airflow calculation for the stated ACH assumption, not a complete HVAC equipment selection.

5. Next checks

After determining airflow, review duct dimensions and velocity, fan performance and pressure loss. For a real project, verify the required ventilation and load calculations and the applicable local requirements.
