Ampacity Calculation: How to Size Conductors Safely

Ampacity is a core part of electrical conductor selection. It describes how much current a conductor can carry under specified conditions without exceeding its permitted temperature.

What is ampacity?

Conductor ampacity is the allowable current capacity of a wire or cable under defined installation conditions. Heat generated by electrical current must be dissipated without exceeding the conductor insulation or termination limits. Ampacity is therefore affected by more than conductor diameter alone.

Why conductor temperature matters

Electrical resistance produces heat as current flows. Higher ambient temperature can reduce the amount of additional heat a conductor can safely dissipate. Conductor insulation also has a temperature rating, and equipment terminals can impose their own limitations. The applicable tables and rules must be checked for the actual installation.

Current-carrying conductors and adjustment

When multiple current-carrying conductors are installed together, heat can accumulate. Electrical rules can require adjustment or derating of allowable ampacity depending on conductor count and installation conditions. The correct adjustment depends on the governing code and the exact wiring arrangement.

Temperature correction

Ambient temperature can require a correction to tabulated ampacity. This is particularly relevant in hot locations, rooftops, mechanical spaces and other environments where conductor temperatures differ from the reference conditions used by standard tables. Use the appropriate correction factors rather than guessing.

Continuous loads

Continuous loads can receive different treatment under electrical codes because sustained current can produce prolonged heating. Determine whether the load qualifies as continuous under the governing rules and apply the required sizing method before selecting the conductor.

Worked sizing example

Suppose a circuit has a calculated design current of 32 A. Begin by identifying the conductor material, insulation and installation method. Select a candidate conductor from the applicable ampacity data, then apply any required temperature and adjustment factors. Finally verify the conductor against the overcurrent protection and equipment terminal ratings.

Ampacity is different from voltage drop

A conductor can have enough ampacity and still have excessive voltage drop on a long run. Ampacity addresses thermal current capacity, while voltage drop addresses the reduction in load voltage caused by conductor impedance. Both checks can influence the final wire size.

Ampacity and breaker sizing

Overcurrent protection and conductor ampacity must be coordinated. A breaker is intended to protect the circuit conductors and equipment under the applicable rules. Do not assume that a breaker rating alone determines the conductor size; the load and installation conditions must be evaluated first.

Common ampacity mistakes

Common errors include using a generic ampacity chart without identifying conductor insulation, ignoring temperature correction, failing to count current-carrying conductors correctly, overlooking terminal ratings and treating ampacity as the only wire-sizing criterion. These mistakes can lead to undersized or incorrectly protected conductors.
