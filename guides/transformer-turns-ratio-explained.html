Transformer Turns Ratio Explained

Learn how transformer turns ratio relates winding turns, voltage and current with practical examples.

1. Turns ratio

For an ideal transformer, Vp/Vs = Np/Ns, relating primary and secondary voltage to winding turns.

2. Step-up and step-down

More secondary turns than primary turns produces a higher secondary voltage in the ideal model; fewer turns produces a lower voltage.

3. Current relationship

For an ideal transformer, power is approximately conserved, so voltage and current move inversely.

4. Example

A 10:1 primary-to-secondary turns ratio with a 240 V primary gives an ideal secondary voltage of about 24 V.

5. Real transformers

Winding resistance, leakage reactance, core losses, temperature and efficiency mean real transformer behavior differs from the ideal model.

Practical takeaway

Use these formulas to understand the calculation and compare scenarios. Verify current legal, tax, engineering-code and project-specific requirements before using an estimate for a real decision.
