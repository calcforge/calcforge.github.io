Ohm's Law Explained

Learn Ohm's Law, V = I × R, with formulas, examples and practical electrical applications.

1. What is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's Law describes the relationship between voltage, current and resistance: V = I × R.

2. Solve for current

Rearranging gives I = V ÷ R. A 120 V source across 24 Ω produces 5 A in the simplified resistive example.

3. Solve for resistance

R = V ÷ I. A 12 V circuit drawing 3 A has an equivalent resistance of 4 Ω.

4. Power relationships

Electrical power is P = V × I. For resistive loads, Ohm's Law also gives P = I²R and P = V²/R.

5. Limits of the model

Motors, semiconductors and other nonlinear devices may not behave like fixed resistors, so operating conditions and manufacturer data matter.
