How to Size a Circuit Breaker

Circuit breaker sizing connects the electrical load, conductor ampacity and overcurrent protection system. This guide explains a practical workflow without treating a generic breaker chart as a substitute for the applicable electrical code.

What a circuit breaker does

A circuit breaker provides overcurrent protection by interrupting a circuit when current exceeds its permitted operating conditions. It is part of a coordinated system that includes conductors, terminals, equipment and the available fault current. The correct breaker is not selected solely from the appliance wattage or from the wire gauge.

Start with load current

Determine the design current of the circuit. If power and voltage are known, current can be estimated from the appropriate electrical relationship, but motors and other equipment may have special requirements. Identify whether the load is continuous, intermittent, motor-related or otherwise subject to specific code provisions.

Match the conductor ampacity

The conductor must be capable of carrying the design load under the actual installation conditions. Conductor ampacity can depend on insulation temperature rating, ambient temperature, conductor count, installation method and terminal limitations. A breaker should protect the conductor rather than simply being chosen because its nominal rating matches a convenient number.

Continuous loads matter

Continuous loads can require additional design margin under applicable electrical rules. The definition of continuous load and the exact calculation method depend on the governing code and equipment type. Always verify the applicable requirement instead of applying a universal percentage to every circuit.

Simple example

Suppose a circuit has a calculated design current of 24 A. The next step is not automatically to install a 25 A or 30 A breaker. First verify the required conductor ampacity and the permitted overcurrent protection under the applicable rules. Then select an available breaker rating that properly coordinates with the conductor and load.

Breaker size versus wire size

Breaker and wire selection are related but not interchangeable. A larger conductor may be needed for voltage drop even when a smaller conductor meets ampacity. Conversely, increasing breaker size without confirming conductor protection can create a dangerous mismatch. Evaluate load current, conductor ampacity and overcurrent protection together.

Motors and special equipment

Motors can have starting current and specific protection rules that differ from ordinary resistive loads. HVAC equipment, welders, transformers and other specialized loads can also have manufacturer-specified circuit protection requirements. For these applications, use the equipment nameplate, manufacturer instructions and applicable code provisions.

Common breaker sizing mistakes

Frequent mistakes include sizing directly from a wire-size chart, ignoring continuous loads, overlooking temperature correction, confusing breaker trip rating with conductor ampacity, and using a calculator output as a code determination. A calculator is useful for estimates and checks, but final design needs authoritative electrical data.

Final verification

Before installation, verify breaker characteristics, interrupting rating, conductor ampacity, equipment ratings, fault-current requirements and local electrical rules. Proper circuit protection is a coordinated design task, not simply a matter of picking the next breaker size above the measured current.
