How to Size a Duct from CFM

Duct sizing starts with the required airflow and a design target for velocity or pressure loss. CFM alone does not define one unique duct size.

1. Establish required CFM

Start with the airflow required at the room or branch. The CFM should come from the design basis, such as a ventilation or load calculation, rather than being guessed from duct dimensions.

2. Select a design velocity

Choose a suitable target velocity based on the application, noise expectations, available static pressure and duct system design. Higher velocity can reduce duct size but may increase pressure loss and noise.

3. Calculate duct area

For a basic velocity method, duct area in square feet can be estimated from area = CFM ÷ velocity in feet per minute. Once area is known, select a practical rectangular or round duct dimension with an equivalent area.

4. Example

If a branch requires 800 CFM and the preliminary target velocity is 1,000 FPM, required area is 800 ÷ 1,000 = 0.80 ft². A practical duct dimension can then be selected near that area, followed by a pressure-loss and system-balance check.

5. Verify the system

A velocity-only calculation is a preliminary sizing method. Final duct design should account for friction, fittings, equivalent length, transitions, filters, coils, dampers, fan static pressure, balancing and applicable design criteria.
